The problem

"We only find out an importer isn't ready when the entry won't go."

"Every new entry, someone re-types the CBP number and the bond number off a PDF. One transposed digit and it's a rejection."

Re-keyed every time

No expiry visibility

"We have three records that all say 'ACME Corp.' Which one does a filing actually use? Nobody's sure."

Duplicate identities

Onboarding a new importer is a checklist someone keeps in their head — legal entity, Form 5106, POA, bond, screening — run once, never surfaced again, and re-discovered the hard way when something lapses.

How Qubere addresses it

Two objects. One clear job each.

  • Client is who you bill and who signs into the portal — payment terms, rate card, invoices, users. Nothing about CBP.
  • Importer is who you file for — legal identity, CBP importer number, Form 5106, power of attorney, customs bond, denied-party screening. One importer belongs to one client.
  • A client can have several importers — a parent and its subsidiaries, each with its own number and bond — or none yet.
  • Every importer carries one readiness light: green when you can file, amber with the exact thing that's missing and a link straight to it.
  • Onboarding isn't a place you leave — it's the guided mode of the importer record. Come back a year later to renew a POA and it's the same three clicks.

Guided onboarding

Five steps, a readiness meter, one clear next action.

Start from a client, pick the path — standard first-time filer, switching brokers, non-resident — and the wizard walks legal entity, Form 5106, POA, bond, and screening in order. Leave and come back; every step saves. The record always tells you what's done and what's next.

  • Switching brokers? The 5106 is an update, and the existing bond is found, not re-entered
  • Non-resident importer? The wizard collects the resident agent and forces a notarized POA

Evidence on the record

Every "verified" has the document behind it.

The bond isn't "verified" because someone typed it — it's verified because the CBP importer/bond query came back with a match, and the raw response is on the record. The POA shows the signer, the method, and the completion certificate. Screening shows every name checked and its result. When an auditor asks "how do you know," the answer is one click away.

  • Bond confirmation, e-signature certificate, screening results — all attached to the importer
  • The same evidence-first standard as the rest of the platform: prove it, don't assert it

Objection handling

Every hard question, answered directly.

"We're moving 200 importers off our old system."Bulk import creates one onboarding record per row. The ones with a valid CBP number and a bond found on file come in ready; the rest land in a triage queue with the exact missing item flagged. Nobody hand-runs 200 wizards.
"One of our clients imports through four legal entities."One client, four importers — each with its own 5106, POA, and bond checklist. The shipment form lets the operator pick which entity; billing still rolls up to the one client.
"What stops someone filing for an importer that isn't ready?"The shipment saves, but transmission is blocked with the specific reason — POA out for signature, bond short, screening not cleared — and a link to fix it. The block is at the entry, not a policy people can forget.
"Can the importer's client change later?"Yes — it's one action, it warns you how many shipments re-associate for billing, and it's logged. Historical invoices are untouched.
