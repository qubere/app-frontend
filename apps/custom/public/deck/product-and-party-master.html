The problem

Your master data assumes one answer to questions that have several.

"We import the same part into the US, the EU, and Mexico. Our 'HS code' field is one value — those are different classifications with different review status."

One code, many jurisdictions

"Origin gets set to whatever the manufacturer's country is. That's not how origin works, and it's a Reasonable Care problem."

Origin ≠ manufacture

"A supplier changed. Nobody re-checked the parts we source from them. Is this party a 'verified supplier'? Depends who you ask."

Stale, ambiguous parties

How Qubere addresses it

Model the facts as they actually are — separate, and each with its own status.

  • One product holds what's true everywhere; a US classification, an EU classification are separate records, each with its own status, reviewer, effective window. Only APPROVED counts.
  • Country of manufacture and country of origin are different facts. Origin is never inferred from a manufacturer, supplier, seller, export, or shipping country.
  • A party has roles (supplier, importer, carrier…) and per-country registration claims, each with its own lifecycle — not one "verified" flag.
  • A name match produces candidates for a human to confirm — never an auto-merge.
  • Change detection closes the loop with screening: a supplier moves → the parties sourced from them get re-screened.

One product, many classifications

A US classification can be approved while the EU one is mid-review — on the same product.

The most important design point for a multi-region import program. A classification is a reviewable case with the GRI analysis, the rulings referenced, and the reviewer's identity — not a code in a field.

  • Each classification: own status, reviewer, effective window
  • Sourcing evidence attached and inspectable

Origin as a fact

Qubere will not fill an origin field to look complete.

Country of manufacture and country of origin are stored as different facts. If there's no approved origin determination, the system says so — and the AI assistant refuses to substitute a manufacturer or supplier country, even when its own fields mention one.

  • A deliberate Reasonable Care stance, enforced end to end
  • Origin determination is a reviewed case with its own evidence

Party master · identity / roles / registrations

A party can be a VERIFIED-registered supplier in one country and an unreviewed identity overall.

Those aren't the same question and Qubere doesn't pretend they are. PartyRole records what a party does; PartyRegistration tracks per-country claims through their own CLAIMED → UNDER_REVIEW → VERIFIED lifecycle — independent of the party's UNREVIEWED → IN_REVIEW → APPROVED status.

  • Each party carries a screening summary (status, last result, staleness)
  • Match → candidates for a human; never auto-merge, never auto-split

One party, every role

The same company, wherever it shows up.

A supplier that later becomes your client's importer of record doesn't start a second life as a new record. It's the same party gaining a role — its screening history, its known aliases, every document already linked to it, all carried forward. Search for the company once and see everything it does for you, not whichever module happened to create it first.

  • Registering an existing party as an importer adds a role — it never duplicates identity
  • The importer's CBP registration, POA, and bond are extensions on the role, the same way a carrier's SCAC and insurance status are

Change detection & CSV import

A supplier moves → the parts you source from them get re-screened → you find out.

Signals that a product's spec, sourcing, or supplier moved in a way that should re-open review — surfaced, not silent. CSV import matches against existing records rather than blindly duplicating, and flags ambiguous matches for a human.

  • Won't silently create 500 duplicates; won't silently merge two real entities
  • Change history drives RDPS re-screening (see Compliance deck)

The master, extended

Everything that hangs off identity is its own managed record.

◇ Importer registration — an importer role on the party: CBP number, POA, and bond, linked to filings
◇ Power of Attorney — POA records per client
◇ Bonds — amount, expiry, sufficiency checks feed filing validation
◇ Clients — the account relationship, auto-routing, portal scope

Assistant reads all of it — get_product, get_party, get_party_history, get_product_origin_position — RBAC-gated, through the same services the screens use.

Objection handling

Every hard question, answered directly.

"We already have an ERP item master / MDM."Qubere isn't replacing the commercial item master — it's the customs master: per-jurisdiction classification, origin determination, sourcing evidence, screening summary. Import from the ERP; be the system of record for the trade attributes the ERP models badly.
"Auto-classification?"AI assists (GRI analysis, ruling references) and can auto-approve above a confidence threshold you set. Assisted, auditable, reversible — never a black box that silently sets the field.
"PEP / beneficial ownership on parties?"Not yet — same known gap as the screening module. Identity, roles, registrations, and screening-summary are what's real.
"If a supplier becomes an importer, do we lose their history?"No — it's the same party gaining a role. Screening history, aliases, and every document link carry forward; nothing restarts from zero.
